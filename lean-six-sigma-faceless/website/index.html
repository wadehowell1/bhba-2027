The problem

You already know where it hurts

You have had this week before.

  • The job that got done twice, because nobody wrote down how it was done the first time.
  • The customer who found the mistake before you did.
  • The quote that was wrong, because the information was in someone's head, or someone's van.
  • The Tuesday that disappeared into questions only you can answer.

None of it is a disaster. That is the problem. It is small, it is constant, and it is expensive in a way that never shows up as a line on the accounts.

And the advice out there does not fit. It assumes a plant, a budget, a spare team and a six-month programme. You have none of those. You have Thursday.

The approach

Fix one process. Properly. Then the next one.

There is a proven method for this. It is called lean, and it works — it is just that almost everything written about it was written for large manufacturers. The published research on why lean fails in small businesses says exactly that: the roadmaps were built for big companies, and small firms cannot run them with the time, money and expertise they actually have. So we changed the packaging, not the method.

How it works

Four weeks, four things done

Each week ends with something finished for your business — not a finished lesson.

  1. Pick and measure

    Choose one process. Write down what actually happens now — not what is supposed to happen.

    You end with: a number you can beat, and a map that fits on one page.

  2. Find the real cause

    Walk the job. Ask the people doing it. Separate the symptoms from the thing actually causing them.

    You end with: a short list of causes, with evidence.

  3. Design and pilot

    Design the new way with the person who does the job. Run it for one week.

    You end with: standard work your team helped write.

  4. Make it stick

    Ten minutes a day to catch problems early. Then measure the same thing again.

    You end with: a before and after, and a process that holds.

Questions

The ones people actually ask

Do I get a certification?

No. We do not sell one and we are not accredited to. If a credential is what you need, Green Belt courses run from a few hundred dollars up to around $2,500 and there are several reputable providers. What we sell is a process that works, which is a different purchase.

Is this just a cost-cutting exercise?

No, and it would not work if it were. The research on why lean fails in small businesses names this exact misreading as a leading cause: the moment a team believes an improvement effort is aimed at their jobs, it stops working. We aim at rework, waiting and mistakes. If your goal is fewer people, we are the wrong people.

I genuinely do not have time.

That is the objection and the symptom. It is also why the scope is what it is: one hour for the Waste Walk, two hours a week after that. If you cannot find two hours a week, this will not work, and we would rather you kept your money.

Why is there nobody's face on this?

Because the material should stand up on its own. The free Waste Walk exists so you can judge the method on your own business before you trust anyone. Coaching is different — you will know exactly who is in the room with you before you pay for anything.

Can I get these templates free somewhere?

Some of them, yes, and there are good free libraries out there. What you are paying for is that ours are filled in, sequenced, and written for a business your size. If a blank template were enough, this would already be fixed.

What results should I expect?

We are not going to give you a number we have not measured. We can tell you the method, the scope, and exactly what you will have at the end of four weeks. When we have measured results from real clients, they will appear on this site with their names on them.

Will this work for my industry?

It works where the same job runs many times a week and runs differently every time. That covers a lot of trades, clinics, agencies, workshops and fulfilment operations. The Waste Walk is free specifically so you can test that on your own process rather than take our word for it.
