# Lean Six Sigma Faceless — Business Plan (Phase 1: Idea)

> **Status:** Draft v1. Every number in this project is an assumption until validated
> against real customers and real costs. Assumptions are labeled inline.

---

## 1. The rough idea (as given)

> Offer lean coaching and digital products around lean, to help small businesses be
> more effective using lean principles. Run as a **faceless** brand.

## 2. The idea, sharpened

Selling "lean" to small businesses has a naming problem and a market problem.

- **Naming problem:** small business owners do not wake up wanting *lean*. They want
  the callbacks to stop, the rework to stop, and to stop being the only person who
  knows how anything gets done.
- **Market problem:** the obvious-looking market — Lean Six Sigma **certification** —
  is crowded and commoditised. Green Belt training already sells from roughly
  **$300 to $2,500** across established providers and universities
  ([sources](research/market-research.md#sources)). Competing there means fighting
  ASQ, IASSC, universities and high-volume training aggregators on price, for a
  customer who wants a credential rather than a fixed business.

**So the concept is not "sell lean training." It is:**

> **Lean, translated for small business.**
> Lean is the engine. The words on the outside are the owner's words — fewer
> callbacks, less rework, a business that runs without them in the room.

We sell **an operating change in one process**, not a belt. Nobody gets certified.
Something gets fixed.

## 3. Problem being solved

Small businesses have processes that technically work and quietly bleed: rework,
callbacks, double entry, chasing, waiting, and "only Dave knows how to do that."
The owner can feel the drag but has no method to find or fix it, and no time to
learn one.

The academic literature on lean in SMEs is unusually blunt about why the existing
answer does not reach them — lack of training and expertise, tiny teams fully
consumed by daily operations, tight budgets, lean being misread as a cost-cutting
(read: layoffs) program, and, critically, that **most published lean roadmaps were
designed for large manufacturers, not for small firms**
([sources](research/market-research.md#sources)).

That last point is the whole opening. The method is proven. The packaging for small
business is missing.

## 4. Target customer (summary — full profile in `/research/ideal-customer-profile.md`)

Two audiences, deliberately, with different roles in the model:

| | **The Operator** (buyer) | **The Practitioner** (reach) |
|---|---|---|
| Who | Owner / GM of a 5–50 person business | Ops manager, CI lead, newly promoted supervisor |
| Pain | Chaos, rework, dependence on them | Told to "improve things," given no method |
| Searches for | "how to stop rework", "SOPs for my team" | "A3 template", "value stream map example", "5S checklist" |
| Buys | Coaching engagement ($$$) | Templates, toolkits ($) |
| Role in model | **Revenue** | **Distribution + low-ticket revenue** |

The Practitioner is reachable through search and short-form video because they use
lean vocabulary. The Operator has the budget but does not use that vocabulary. The
model uses practitioner-intent content to build the audience and low-ticket products,
and outcome-language content to convert operators into coaching.

> **⚠ Biggest assumption in the entire business.** That these two audiences overlap
> enough — that practitioner-intent traffic contains, or can reach, people with
> operator budget. If it does not, the business splits into a low-margin template
> shop and a coaching practice with no lead source. **Validate this first.** See
> `/research/validation-plan.md`.

## 5. Proposed solution

A four-rung ladder, from free to done-with-you:

1. **Free — The 60-Minute Waste Walk.** A guided one-hour audit of a single process.
   Genuinely useful alone. Lead magnet.
2. **Low ticket — The Small Shop Lean Kit.** The lean toolset rewritten in small
   business language: SIPOC, A3, value stream map, 5S, standard work, daily huddle,
   DMAIC tracker. Google Sheets / Docs, not PowerPoint theory.
3. **Mid ticket — Fix One Process.** A self-paced 30-day system that takes one real
   process end to end. The product is the finished process, not the lessons.
4. **High ticket — The 90-Day Coaching Engagement.** Done-with-you. Cohort or 1:1.

Pricing in `/offer/pricing-worksheet.md`. All figures assumptions.

## 6. Business model

Hybrid: **digital products (one-time, scalable)** funding and feeding
**coaching (retainer/engagement, high margin, capacity-limited)**.

Deliberately *not* certification, *not* a marketplace, *not* software.

## 7. Core value proposition

> Most lean material was written for factories with a thousand people.
> This is lean rewritten for a business with eleven — where the person fixing the
> process is also the person answering the phone.

Fix one process at a time. Keep the result. Repeat.

## 8. Why "faceless" is a strategic fit (and where it hurts)

**Fits because:**
- Production is systematised, not performed — publish consistently without on-camera
  talent, which is the documented advantage of the format for B2B education content
  ([sources](research/market-research.md#sources)).
- The subject matter is inherently visual and non-personal: diagrams, boards,
  before/after process maps, screen recordings of templates. Lean explains better on
  a whiteboard than on a face.
- It is sellable/transferable later. A brand asset, not a personal brand.
- It survives the founder having a day job.

**Hurts because:**
- **Trust is the whole sale in coaching.** People hire a person to change how their
  business runs. A faceless brand can sell a $49 template far more easily than a
  $2,000 engagement.
- No founder story, no face-led social proof, harder to get podcast/referral traction.

**Resolution (recommended):** *faceless brand, not anonymous human.* The brand
publishes facelessly — no face on camera, no personality-led content. But the
coaching tier names a real, credentialed human, and discovery calls are with that
human. Faceless is the **marketing** posture, not the **delivery** posture.

> **⚠ Needs human decision.** If the intent is genuinely anonymous — including
> delivery — then the high-ticket coaching tier should be cut and the business
> rebuilt as products-only. That is a viable business, but a different one. See
> `/launch/human-review.md`.

## 10. Biggest assumptions

| # | Assumption | Risk if wrong |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | Practitioner audience and operator budget overlap | Model breaks in half |
| A2 | Small businesses will pay for process fixing, not credentials | No mid/high tier |
| A3 | A faceless brand can convert high-ticket coaching | Ladder caps at low ticket |
| A4 | Outcome language outperforms lean jargon for the operator | Wrong content strategy |
| A5 | One-process-at-a-time scope is small enough to say yes to | Long sales cycles |
| A6 | Templates are differentiated enough vs. free/cheap alternatives | Commodity pricing |

## 11. Biggest risks

- **Commoditisation.** Lean templates are already sold cheaply on Etsy and given away
  free by content sites. Differentiation must be *translation and specificity*, not
  the artifacts themselves.
- **Credibility gap.** Selling improvement methodology without a named practitioner
  invites "who are you?" — the hardest objection to answer facelessly.
- **No proof at launch.** Zero case studies on day one, and the honesty rules of this
  project forbid inventing them. Early pricing must account for buying proof.
- **Founder capacity.** Coaching does not scale; products do not sell themselves.
- **Owner attention.** The buyer is busy in the business, which is the exact reason
  they need this and the exact reason they will not read a 40-page PDF.

## 12. What needs to be validated (before building anything else)

Ranked, with method, in `/research/validation-plan.md`. Short version:

1. Do practitioner-intent viewers contain operator-budget buyers? (A1)
2. Will an operator pay for a process outcome with no certificate attached? (A2)
3. Does the faceless brand survive a high-ticket sales conversation? (A3)

## 13. Recommended next step

**Do not build the full ladder yet.** Build the free Waste Walk and the low-ticket
Kit, publish practitioner-intent content, and use the email list to test A1 and A2
with 15–20 real conversations before writing a single lesson of the mid-tier course.
