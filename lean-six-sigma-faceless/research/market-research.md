## A. VERIFIED INFORMATION

### A1. Certification training is an established, priced, crowded market

Published Lean Six Sigma Green Belt training prices span roughly **$300 to $2,500**:

- Self-paced / aggregator online courses commonly listed from **~$300** upward.
- University-run programs are materially higher — the University of Kansas lists an
  in-person 5-day Green Belt at **$2,495** and an 8-week online self-paced Green Belt
  at **$1,995**.
- Certification **exam** fees sit separately at around **$295**, with an ASQ Green Belt
  re-attempt fee of **$269**, and IASSC renewal at **$175**.

**What this tells us:** the customer's reference price for "learning lean" is already
anchored between a few hundred and a few thousand dollars — and what they get for it
is a *credential*. Any offer priced in that band will be mentally compared to a
certification unless it is positioned somewhere else entirely.

**Strategic read:** do not sell a competing certification. There is no wedge there —
the incumbents own the accreditation, the exam bodies, and the search results.

### A2. Lean genuinely under-serves small firms — and this is documented

Peer-reviewed and academic literature on lean in SMEs consistently identifies the same
barriers:

- **Training and expertise gaps** — poor training is repeatedly named a primary cause
  of failed implementation; skills and knowledge are a considerable impediment.
- **Structural constraints** — small teams, tight budgets, little time for training,
  and a workforce fully consumed by day-to-day operations, which makes changing those
  operations difficult.
- **Management and cultural barriers** — lack of top-management commitment, middle
  management resistance, weak employee involvement, over-reliance on tools, and the
  perception that **lean is a cost-cutting program rather than an improvement
  philosophy**.
- **Framework mismatch** — many published lean roadmaps were built for **large
  manufacturing companies, not for SMEs**, and firms struggle to apply them.

**What this tells us:** this is the single strongest piece of evidence in the file.
The gap is not "small businesses don't need lean." The gap is "the existing lean
product was not built for them." That is a positioning gift, and it is documented
rather than asserted.

**Direct implications for the offer:**
1. Scope must be tiny (small teams, no slack time) → *one process at a time*.
2. Language must avoid cost-cutting connotations → talk about rework and chaos,
   never headcount.
3. Deliverables must be usable without expertise → filled-in examples, not blank
   templates.

### A3. A low-cost template/toolkit market already exists

Lean Six Sigma templates and toolkits are actively sold as digital downloads on Etsy —
including a "Six Sigma Project Template Starter Pack" (data collection plan,
stakeholder analysis, VOC, SIPOC-S) marketed as built by a Master Black Belt, and a
"Lean 6 Sigma Toolkit" marketed as ex-McKinsey/Deloitte/BCG authored, bundling
~330 PowerPoint slides, 21 Excel sheets and an hour of video.

Comprehensive lean template libraries are also given away **free** by content sites
(e.g. Learn Lean Sigma).

**What this tells us:** template supply is real, it is competitive, and a meaningful
share of it is free. Selling "the templates" is selling a commodity.

**Strategic read:** the artifact is not the product. **Translation is the product** —
small-business-specific wording, worked examples from an 11-person business rather
than a 1,100-person plant, and a sequence that tells the buyer what to do on Monday.

### A4. Faceless content is a recognized, working format for B2B education

Industry commentary on the format is consistent: for businesses the highest-return
faceless formats are educational explainers, walkthroughs and training content; the
approach turns video into a repeatable production system instead of a performance,
and keeps attention on the message — which suits B2B audiences. Decoupling the channel
from one spokesperson allows consistent shipping and an evergreen library.

**[weak source]** Several vendor blogs claim B2B/finance faceless niches earn among
the highest RPMs (figures of **$8–$18** are quoted). These are AI-video-tool vendors
publishing content marketing, with no methodology disclosed. **Treat as directional
only — do not build a revenue model on it.** Ad revenue is in any case not part of
this business model.

## C. QUESTIONS THAT STILL NEED VALIDATION

1. **C1 — Retainer pricing is not public.** Multiple lean consultancies advertise
   monthly retainer and fractional-support models for SMEs, but **none publish
   prices**. Our entire high-ticket price point is therefore unanchored to observed
   market data. *Method: 5–8 competitor enquiry calls to obtain real quotes.*
2. **C2 — Actual Etsy/Gumroad price points and sales volumes** for lean toolkits were
   not retrievable from search result pages. *Method: manual listing review; capture
   price and "sales" counts for 15 listings.*
3. **C3 — Keyword demand.** No search-volume data has been gathered for either
   practitioner terms ("A3 template", "5S checklist") or operator terms ("stop
   rework", "systemize my business"). *Method: keyword tool pass before committing
   the content calendar.*
4. **C4 — Which vertical.** Trades, clinics, agencies, e-commerce ops and light
   manufacturing all fit the profile. Not yet chosen. *Method: 15 discovery
   conversations, scored on pain, budget and reachability.*
5. **C5 — Willingness to pay** at every rung. Nothing tested.
6. **C6 — Are they already buying something else** for this problem (an EOS
   implementer, an SOP consultant, a fractional COO, a $200/mo SOP SaaS)? The real
   competitor may not be a lean provider at all. *Method: ask in every discovery call
   — "what have you already tried and what did it cost?"*

## D. COMPETITOR LANDSCAPE

Grouped by what they actually take money for, not by what they call themselves.

| Group | Examples observed | Strength | Weakness for our buyer |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Accreditation bodies** | ASQ, IASSC, CSSC | Own the credential; trusted | Sell exams, not outcomes; nothing changes in the business |
| **Training aggregators** | KnowledgeHut, Unichrone, Master of Project | Volume, SEO dominance, low price | Generic, enterprise-flavored, learner-focused |
| **University programs** | e.g. University of Kansas ($1,995–$2,495) | Institutional credibility | Expensive, scheduled, time-costly for an owner |
| **Lean consultancies** | New England Lean Consulting, Incito, OpEx Lean, Mattox, GENEDGE | Real implementation; retainers | Priced/scoped for mid-market manufacturers; opaque pricing (C1) |
| **Free content libraries** | Learn Lean Sigma | Free, comprehensive | No sequence, no accountability, no translation |
| **Template sellers** | Etsy toolkit listings | Cheap, instant | Slide decks; enterprise framing; no support |
| **Adjacent (likely the real rival)** | EOS implementers, fractional COOs, SOP consultants | Speak the owner's language already | Not method-driven; often pricier |

### Competitor weaknesses we can actually exploit

1. **Everyone sells to the learner; almost nobody sells the fixed process.**
2. **Enterprise framing throughout** — examples are plants and hospitals, not an
   11-person shop.
3. **Scope is too big.** Consultancies open with an assessment; owners want one thing
   fixed.
4. **Opaque pricing** in the consulting tier — an opportunity to publish prices and
   win the buyer who refuses to "book a call to find out."

### Common customer complaints (hypothesis — **needs C2/C6 evidence**)

Not yet evidenced from review mining. To be filled from Etsy/Udemy/G2 reviews and
r/leanmanufacturing, r/smallbusiness threads. Expected themes, to be confirmed or

example", "certificate was worthless to my employer".

## E. MARKET GAPS IDENTIFIED

1. **The translation gap** — proven method, no small-business packaging (evidenced, A2).
2. **The scope gap** — nothing sold at "one process, 30 days, done."
3. **The price-transparency gap** — implementation help is all "contact us."
4. **The worked-example gap** — templates are blank; the buyer lacks the expertise to
   fill them (A2 training-gap finding + A3 commodity supply).

---

## F. VERDICT

### **TEST MORE** — do not go, do not rethink.

**Why not GO:** the two load-bearing beams (B1 practitioner↔operator overlap, B3
high-ticket price) have **zero** supporting evidence. C1 means we could not even
observe what competitors charge. Building the full ladder now is building on air.

**Why not RETHINK:** the underlying gap is real and independently documented (A2), the
market demonstrably spends money in this space (A1, A3), and the positioning wedge —
translation, not certification — is defensible.

**The cheap test, in order:** publish practitioner-intent content and the free Waste
Walk → build a list → run 15–20 discovery conversations against C4/C5/C6 → obtain 5–8
competitor quotes for C1 → *then* price and build the mid and high tiers.
