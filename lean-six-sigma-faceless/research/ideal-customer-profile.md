# Ideal Customer Profile

Two profiles. The **Operator** is who we sell the engagement to. The **Practitioner**
is who we reach first. Confusing the two is the fastest way to kill this business.

> **Evidence marking:** ✅ = supported by the research in `market-research.md`.
> 🔶 = inference, reasoned but unevidenced. ❓ = pure guess, needs a conversation.

---

## PROFILE 1 — "The Operator" (the buyer)

### WHO THEY ARE
Owner, founder or general manager of a business with roughly **5–50 employees**.
🔶 Typically 3–15 years in. Past survival, short of systems. Competent at the craft
the business sells; self-taught at running it. Usually still doing a frontline job
two days a week.

### JOB / BUSINESS
🔶 Owner-operator of a service or light-production business with **repeatable work**:
trades and home services, clinics (dental, veterinary, physio), agencies and studios,
e-commerce fulfilment, small food production, specialist workshops.

**The qualifier is not the industry, it is the shape of the work:** the same job runs
many times a week, and it runs differently every time.

> ❓ **Vertical is not yet chosen — see C4.** Picking one beats spreading across five.

### MAIN PROBLEM
✅ The business depends on them personally, and the same faults recur: rework,
callbacks, jobs quoted wrong, things falling between people, and the one employee
who is the only person who knows how something works.

✅ They have no method for this, no time to learn one, and no slack in the team to
absorb a change programme. The literature is explicit that SMEs are consumed by
day-to-day operations and cannot easily change those operations.

### WHAT THEY WANT
- The same job to come out the same way every time.
- To stop being the bottleneck and the escalation point.
- 🔶 To take a two-week holiday without the business degrading.
- To grow without the chaos growing proportionally.

They do **not** want: a certificate, a philosophy, a transformation programme, or a
binder.

### WHAT THEY HAVE ALREADY TRIED
🔶 Hiring someone senior and hoping they would "sort out ops." Buying software
(a CRM, a job management tool, a project tool) and using 20% of it. Writing SOPs
once, in a burst, which nobody reads now. Reading *The E-Myth* or *Traction*, and
agreeing with it. 🔶 Possibly one bad consultant.

> ❓ Confirm in discovery. C6 matters more than any other question: **what did they
> already pay for, and what did it cost?** Our real competitor may be an EOS
> implementer or a fractional COO, not a lean provider.

### WHAT THEY DISLIKE
- ✅ **Anything that smells like cost-cutting or layoffs.** The literature names this
  directly as a cultural barrier — lean read as a cost programme rather than an
  improvement one. Say "efficiency" carelessly and the team hears "redundancies."
- ✅ Enterprise material. Case studies about Toyota and 900-person plants read as
  "not for me."
- Jargon. DMAIC, kaizen, muda, poka-yoke, gemba — all of it, on first contact.
- Blank templates. ✅ They lack the expertise to fill them in; that is the training
  gap the research identifies.
- "Book a call to discuss pricing."
- Long programmes with a payoff in month six.

### WHAT THEY WOULD PAY FOR
🔶 A specific, bounded fix to a named, painful process — with the artifact left behind
and their team actually using it. Paying for *the thing being done*, alongside them,
not for lessons about doing it.

> ❓ Price point entirely unvalidated (B3, C5).

### MAIN OBJECTION
**"I don't have time for this."** — which is the objection *and* the symptom. They are
too busy for the only thing that would make them less busy.

Secondary: **"Who are you?"** — sharper than usual against a faceless brand (B6).
Tertiary: "My business is different."

### PURCHASE TRIGGER
🔶 A visible, expensive failure: a big customer lost to a preventable mistake, a
costly rework month, a key employee resigning and taking undocumented knowledge with
them, or a growth event (new site, big contract) that exposes the lack of process.

**Implication:** this is trigger-driven, not steady-demand. The content library must
be there *before* the trigger, so they find it when it hits. This is an argument for
evergreen search content over campaign bursts.

## PROFILE 2 — "The Practitioner" (the reach)

### WHO THEY ARE
🔶 An ops manager, quality lead, CI coordinator or recently promoted supervisor, aged
mid-20s to 40s, at a small or mid-size firm. Sometimes an owner's second-in-command.
Sometimes studying for a belt.

### JOB / BUSINESS
Responsible for "making things run better," typically without authority, budget or
training. ✅ The training/expertise gap is well documented.

### MAIN PROBLEM
They have been handed an improvement mandate and no method. They need something
usable **this week**, in front of a manager who wants results.

### WHAT THEY WANT
A template that is already filled in. An example they can copy. Language that makes
them sound competent in Thursday's meeting.

### WHAT THEY HAVE ALREADY TRIED
✅ Free template libraries (comprehensive but unsequenced), cheap Etsy toolkits
(✅ enterprise-framed slide decks), YouTube explainers, and possibly a Green Belt
course (✅ $300–$2,500).

### WHAT THEY DISLIKE
Theory without a worked example. 300-slide decks. Material assuming a plant, a budget
and an executive sponsor.

### WHAT THEY WOULD PAY FOR
🔶 **$20–$80** for a toolkit that is genuinely done-for-them and small-business
shaped. Note the ceiling: this is a personal-wallet or petty-cash purchase, not a
budget line.

### MAIN OBJECTION
"I can find this free." ✅ **Largely true** — free comprehensive libraries exist. The
only honest answer is that ours is filled in, sequenced, and written for their size
of business.

### PURCHASE TRIGGER
Being assigned a specific improvement project with a deadline.

## How the two connect (the model's load-bearing beam)

```
        Practitioner-intent content            Operator-intent content
        "A3 template", "5S checklist"          "stop rework", "runs without me"
                     │                                    │
                     ▼                                    ▼
              free Waste Walk  ──────►  email list  ◄──── free Waste Walk
                     │                       │
                     ▼                       ▼
                 $49 Kit                 discovery call
                     │                       │
                     └──────► ??? ───────────┘
                          (A1 / B2)
                     ▼
              $349 Fix One Process  ──►  $2,400 / 90-day coaching
```

The `???` is the assumption the whole business rests on: that practitioner traffic
either **contains** operators, or **routes to** them (the practitioner recommends us
internally to the owner who signs).

**Two ways it can be true, and they need different content:**
1. *Direct* — small-business owners themselves search lean terms. Cheap to test (C3).
2. *Referral* — the practitioner is a champion who brings us to their owner. This is
   a real, common B2B motion, but it needs deliberate design: material the
   practitioner can forward, and a reason for them to forward it.

> **If neither holds, do not scale the content engine.** Sell coaching by direct
> outreach and treat products as a separate, smaller business.

## Segments to explicitly EXCLUDE at launch

Saying no here is what makes the content specific enough to work.

| Excluded | Why |
|---|---|
| Enterprise / 500+ headcount | Already served; procurement cycles; not our packaging |
| Solo operators / freelancers | Process problems are real but budget is not |
| Belt-seekers | They want a credential; we do not sell one — refer them out |
| Pre-revenue startups | No repeatable process to improve yet |
| Anyone wanting headcount reduction | ✅ Wrong framing; poisons delivery and reputation |
