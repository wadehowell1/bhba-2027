# Validation Plan

The market research verdict was **TEST MORE**. This is the test. It is designed to be
runnable in ~4 weeks alongside a job, for well under $500.

**Rule:** each test has a pre-committed pass/fail threshold, written *before* the test
runs. Deciding what counts as success after seeing the result is how founders talk
themselves into building things nobody buys.

---

## Test 1 — Does anyone search for this? (validates B7, C3)

**Cost:** free–$50 (one month of a keyword tool) · **Time:** 3 hours

Pull search volumes for two baskets:

| Practitioner basket | Operator basket |
|---|---|
| a3 template, 5s checklist, value stream map example, dmaic template, standard work template, root cause analysis template | how to stop rework, sop template small business, my business can't run without me, reduce mistakes in my team, systemize my business |

**Pass:** the operator basket shows meaningful non-branded volume → the direct path is
live; build operator-intent content.
**Fail:** operator basket is near zero → **the referral path is the only path.** Content
targets practitioners, and the funnel must be redesigned so practitioners forward us
to owners.
**Either way this is not a kill signal** — it is a fork. Run it first because it is the
cheapest and it changes everything downstream.

---

## Test 2 — Competitor price discovery (validates B3, C1)

**Cost:** free · **Time:** 4–6 hours over two weeks

Contact 5–8 lean/CI consultancies serving SMEs as a genuine prospective buyer,
describing a real business shape. Ask directly for scope and price.

Record: entry engagement price, retainer price, minimum term, minimum company size.

**Pass:** we find a defensible position under the market's entry price with a
tighter scope.
**Fail:** entry engagements cluster far below our $2,400 assumption → reprice.
**Also fail:** nobody will quote without a lengthy sales process → confirms the
price-transparency gap is real and worth attacking (this is a *good* failure).

> ⚖️ **Be straight with them.** Ask as yourself, exploring the market. Do not
> impersonate a fake company, do not waste a genuine sales team's afternoon under
> false pretences, and do not take a quote you have no intention of considering
> without saying so. The information is obtainable honestly.

---

## Test 3 — The Waste Walk landing test (validates demand for the free tier)

**Cost:** $0 organic, or ~$100 paid · **Time:** 1 week live

Publish the landing page and the lead magnet. Drive traffic from organic posts in
communities where the audience already is, and optionally a small paid test.

**Pre-committed thresholds:**
- Landing page → email opt-in: **≥ 25%** (a focused, single-purpose page should clear
  this; below 15% means the offer or the audience is wrong).
- Opt-in → actually completes the Waste Walk (measured by the reply prompt at the end):
  **≥ 15%**.

**Fail:** the promise is not compelling, or the audience is wrong. Do not proceed to
paid tiers.

---

## Test 4 — 15 discovery conversations (validates B1, B2, A2 fit, C4, C5, C6)

**The single most important test. Nothing below the free tier should be built until
this is done.**

**Cost:** free · **Time:** 2–3 weeks

Recruit from the email list and from direct outreach. Target ≥ 8 Operators and ≥ 5
Practitioners.

### Script (do not pitch — this is not a sales call)

1. Walk me through the last time a job went wrong. What actually happened?
2. What did that cost you — money, time, or the customer?
3. How often does that kind of thing happen?
4. What have you already tried to fix it? *(→ C6)*
5. What did that cost, and why did you stop? *(→ C6, C5)*
6. Who else, if anyone, worries about this in your business? *(→ B2 referral path)*
7. If it were fixed, what would be different on a Tuesday?
8. *Last, and only last:* here is what I'm considering building. What's wrong with it?

**Rules:** ask about the past, not the future — "would you buy" answers are worthless.
Do not describe the offer until question 8. Count the number who ask "how much?"
unprompted; that is the strongest signal in the whole exercise.

**Pass:** ≥ 8 of 15 describe a recurring, costed, painful process failure **and** ≥ 4
ask about price or availability unprompted.
**Fail:** the pain is real but nobody has ever paid to fix it → likely a
"vitamin, not painkiller" business at this price point. Reconsider before building
the paid tiers.

---

## Test 5 — Sell the Kit before finishing it (validates B4, C5)

**Cost:** $0–$29 (payment processing setup) · **Time:** 1 week

Build the Kit's contents **first** (they are needed regardless), then sell it at $49
to the list with a real checkout. Fulfil immediately.

**Pass:** ≥ 2% of the list buys within 7 days.
**Fail below 1%:** the low tier is not a business — either the list is the wrong
audience (back to Test 1's fork) or the translation promise is not landing.

> **Do not pre-sell something that does not exist yet with a delayed delivery date.**
> Either it is ready or it is not for sale.

---

## Test 6 — One paid pilot engagement (validates B1, B3, B6 — the whole high tier)

**Cost:** your time · **Time:** 90 days

Sell **one** coaching engagement, at a stated discount, explicitly as a pilot, to a
business found through Test 4. Deliver it properly. Measure a real before/after on one
process. Ask for permission to publish it as the first case study.

**Pass:** they pay, the process measurably improves, and they agree to be referenced.
**This single result unlocks everything** — it is the first honest proof the business
has, and the proof problem is currently the biggest constraint on the whole ladder.
**Fail:** you cannot sell one at a discount to someone who already told you the pain
is real → the high tier does not exist yet. Stay products-only.

## Sequence and gates

```
Test 1 (keywords) ──► fork: direct vs referral content strategy
        │
Test 2 (competitor pricing) ──► sets the high-tier price band
        │
        ▼
Test 3 (landing + lead magnet)  ──►  GATE 1: ≥25% opt-in
        │
        ▼
Test 4 (15 conversations)       ──►  GATE 2: ≥8 costed pains, ≥4 price questions
        │                              ⛔ Nothing paid gets built before this gate
        ▼
Test 5 (sell the Kit)           ──►  GATE 3: ≥2% conversion
        │
        ▼
Test 6 (one pilot engagement)   ──►  GATE 4: paid, improved, referenceable
        │
        ▼
   Build the mid tier (Fix One Process) — and not before
```

**The mid-tier course is the most expensive thing to build and the last thing to
build.** That ordering is deliberate: it is a large content investment that only pays
off if Gates 2–4 have already shown that people buy the outcome.

---

## What would make us stop entirely

Written down now, while it is still cheap to be honest:

- Gate 2 fails and re-running with a different vertical also fails → the pain is not
  paid-for at this size of business.
- Test 2 shows entry engagements at a price we cannot profitably undercut while
  delivering real work.
- Six months of content produces an audience that engages but never buys anything at
  any tier.
