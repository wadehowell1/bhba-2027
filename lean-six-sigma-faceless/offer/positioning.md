# Positioning & Brand

## The positioning statement

> For the owner of a small business where the same job comes out differently every
> time, **The Lean Desk** is a set of tools and coaching that fixes one process at a
> time — so it runs the same way without you in the room.
> Unlike certification courses and enterprise lean consultancies, we sell the fixed
> process, not a credential or a transformation program.

## The one-line version

**Lean, translated for small business.**

## The wedge, stated plainly

The lean method is proven and the material is abundant. What is missing — and this is
documented in the SME lean literature, not asserted by us — is that **the existing
frameworks were built for large manufacturers**, and small firms cannot apply them
with the time, budget and expertise they actually have.

So the product is not the method. **The product is the translation.**

Three things that translation means concretely:
1. **Scope shrunk to fit** — one process, 30 days, two hours a week.
2. **Language changed** — the owner's words on the outside, lean underneath.
3. **Examples replaced** — an 11-person business, not a 1,100-person plant; every
   template filled in, because the buyer does not have the expertise to fill in a
   blank one.

## Naming

**Recommended: The Lean Desk**

Why it works for a faceless brand: "the desk" reads as an institution rather than a
person — the same way a news desk or a help desk does. It carries authority without a
face, which is exactly the problem a faceless brand has to solve. It is short, says
the category, and works as a channel name, a domain and a product prefix
("the 90-Day Desk").

**Alternatives considered**

| Name | For | Against |
|---|---|---|
| Small Shop Lean | Instantly says who it is for | Sounds small; harder to grow upmarket |
| The Improvement Desk | Avoids the word "lean" entirely | Vaguer; loses the search advantage |
| Standard Work Co. | Practitioners respect the term | Jargon to the operator — wrong door |
| Waste Not Ops | Memorable | "Waste" reads as cost-cutting — ✅ the exact misreading the research warns about |
| Tuesday Ops | Distinctive, ownable | Says nothing about the category |

> ⚠️ **Human review required — do this before anything is printed.**
> Domain availability, trademark search in your jurisdiction, social handle
> availability, and a check that no established lean consultancy already trades under
> a confusingly similar name. **None of this has been checked.**

## Voice

**Sound like:** a competent operator explaining something to another operator, in a
yard, with limited time. Short sentences. Concrete nouns. Numbers when we have them,
silence when we do not.

**Never sound like:** a certification body, a LinkedIn thought-leader, a
transformation consultancy, or an ad.

**Word list**

| Say | Don't say |
|---|---|
| the same way every time | standardization |
| rework, doing it twice | non-conformance |
| where the time goes | value stream analysis |
| a one-pager | an A3 |
| walk the job | gemba walk |
| stop the mistake happening | poka-yoke |
| the daily ten minutes | tiered accountability huddle |

The technical terms are taught **inside** the products, in the Translation Guide —
because practitioners need the vocabulary and it earns credibility. They are just not
what greets the operator at the door.

## The one rule for every piece of content

Say nothing we cannot show. No results we have not measured, no clients we do not
have, no savings we have not seen. Until Test 6 produces a real case study, the proof
is the free Waste Walk working on the reader's own business — and that is a stronger
proof than a testimonial anyway.
