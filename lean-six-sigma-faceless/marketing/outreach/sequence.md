# Outreach Sequence

**Purpose:** the first coaching clients will not come from content. The audience is not
built yet, and coaching is a trust sale. It comes from talking to people.

**Assume the reader is a real, busy person.** Every message below is short enough to
read on a phone between jobs.

---

## Before you send anything

**Rules that are not optional**

1. **Research first.** If you cannot name something specific about their business, do
   not send. Generic mail-merge is why this channel has a bad name.
2. **Send to a business address**, about their business. ⚠️ Unsolicited B2B email is
   regulated differently by jurisdiction (GDPR/PECR, CAN-SPAM, CASL). **Check your
   obligations before the first send.** Some jurisdictions require an opt-out route and
   accurate sender identity in every message; some require more.
3. **No fake familiarity.** Not "following up on our conversation" if there was none.
   Not "I noticed you're hiring" if you did not.
4. **Four messages, then stop.** Someone who has not replied to four is not
   interested, and a fifth converts nothing except goodwill into irritation.
5. **Honour "no" instantly and warmly.** Small industries talk.

**Who to contact:** businesses matching the ICP where you have a genuine reason to
reach out — a visible process problem, a local presence, a mutual connection, or a
trade community you actually participate in.

---

## Message 1 — First contact

**Subject:** quick question about your booking process

> Hi [name],
>
> I work with [trade/sector] businesses on the boring operational stuff — the jobs that
> get done twice, the quotes that go out wrong, the things only one person knows how to
> do.
>
> Not pitching you anything. I'm having conversations with about fifteen owners of
> [sector] firms this month to understand where the time actually goes, because most
> of the material written about this assumes a factory with a thousand staff and I'm
> trying to build something that fits a business your size.
>
> Twenty minutes, and I'll send you the one-hour audit I use either way — it's free
> and it works on its own.
>
> Worth a conversation?
>
> [name]

**Why this works — and where it is fragile.** It is true (Test 4 is a real research
exercise), it asks for twenty minutes rather than a sale, and it gives something
whether or not they say yes. It is fragile because "not pitching you anything" must
be *true* — if the call turns into a pitch, you have burned them and anyone they talk
to.

---

## Follow-up 1 *(4 days later)*

**Subject:** re: quick question

> Hi [name],
>
> Adding one detail in case it's more useful than my first message.
>
> The pattern I keep finding: the job takes an hour, the process takes three days, and
> almost all the difference is waiting — sat in an inbox, waiting on a decision,
> waiting on a callback. Which means the usual instinct, get people working faster,
> barely moves anything.
>
> Still happy to send the audit whether or not we talk. Want it?
>
> [name]

*Give something in the follow-up. A follow-up that only says "just bumping this" asks
for attention and offers nothing.*

---

## Follow-up 2 *(8 days after that)*

**Subject:** the waiting thing

> Hi [name],
>
> Last useful thing from me, then I'll leave you alone.
>
> If you want to test this yourself in an hour without talking to anyone: pick the
> process that annoyed you most this week, watch one real run of it, and write down
> every step and how long each one waits before it starts. The gap between the time
> the work takes and the time the process takes is usually the whole answer.
>
> Here's the worksheet version: [link]
>
> [name]

---

## Breakup message *(10 days after that)*

**Subject:** closing the loop

> Hi [name],
>
> I'll assume the timing isn't right and stop emailing — no reply needed.
>
> The audit link stays live if it's ever useful: [link]
>
> If things change, or you'd rather I spoke to someone else there, just say.
>
> Good luck with it,
> [name]

**No guilt, no "I guess you're not interested in growing your business," no final
discount.** The breakup message gets more replies than any of the others precisely
because it asks for nothing.

## Tracking

| Field | Why |
|---|---|
| Company, contact, source | Basic |
| Date of each message sent | Enforces the gaps |
| Replied? (Y/N/positive/negative) | Reply rate is the metric that matters |
| Call booked? | The actual goal |
| **What they said the problem was** | **The most valuable column — this is Test 4 data** |
| What they already pay for | Answers C6 |
| Outcome | Closes the loop |

> That "what they said the problem was" column is the real product of outreach in the
> first three months. Even a 5% booking rate is worth it if the other 95% of replies
> tell you what language the market uses.

## Realistic expectations

⚠️ No response-rate numbers are given here on purpose. Published cold-outreach
benchmarks vary wildly by list quality, sector and jurisdiction, and quoting one would
be inventing a forecast. Send your first 40, measure your own rate, and use that.
