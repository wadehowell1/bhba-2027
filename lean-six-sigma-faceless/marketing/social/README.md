# Faceless Social — TikTok & Instagram

The social engine for **The Lean Desk**. Faceless by design, slideshow-first by
decision, and built so a week of content can be approved in one sitting and publish
itself.

---

## The strategic bet, stated plainly

**Small business owners are on TikTok and Instagram. Lean content is not.**

Search TikTok for lean or continuous improvement and you get corporate training clips,
certification ads, and factory footage — all speaking to practitioners inside large
companies. Meanwhile the actual audience, owner-operators of 5–50 person businesses,
is on the same platforms consuming trades content, "day in the business" content and
small-business commentary, in enormous volume.

The gap is the same one the market research found, showing up on a different surface:
**the method exists, the packaging for this audience does not.**

> ⚠️ **This is a bet, not a finding.** No keyword or hashtag volume has been verified.
> The first 30 days *are* the test — see "How we will know it is working" below.

## Why faceless works here — and the one place it hurts

**Works:** the content is inherently visual and non-personal. A process map, a
before/after board, a filled-in template, a number on a screen. None of it needs a
face, and a face would arguably get in the way. It is also the only format that
survives being produced in batches by one person with a day job.

**Hurts:** trust. Nobody buys a $2,400 engagement from an anonymous slideshow account.

**How we resolve it:** social is the top of the funnel and nothing more. Its job is to
get the right person onto the email list, where a named human takes over. The account
never has to close a sale. It has to be *useful enough to follow*.

## Format decision: slideshows and carousels first

| | |
|---|---|
| **TikTok** | Photo mode (slideshow), 6–9 slides |
| **Instagram** | Carousel, 1080×1350, same content |

**Why this rather than video:**

1. **It closes the automation loop.** No rendering step, no voiceover, no editing.
   Assets can be generated and published end to end, which is the entire point of the
   approval-once-a-week system.
2. **It suits the content.** This material is text and diagrams. A slideshow is the
   native form of "here are the five places the quote goes wrong."
3. **It is saveable.** Carousels and slideshows get saved and sent to a colleague far
   more than talking-head video does — and "sends to a business partner" is exactly the
   behavior that reaches an owner with budget.
4. **It is cheap to be wrong.** If a format fails, we have lost an hour, not a
   weekend of filming.

**Video is phase two**, once a pillar has proven it can hold attention. The scripts in
the calendar are written so any post can be re-cut as a 45-second screen recording
later without rewriting.

## Content pillars

Five pillars, in rotation. Each has a different job.

| # | Pillar | Job | Frequency |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **The Callout** | Name a pain so precisely they think you have been in their business | ~30% |
| **2** | **The Translation** | Lean term → plain English. The differentiator, and inherently saveable | ~20% |
| **3** | **The Fix** | One tactical thing they can do this week | ~25% |
| **4** | **The Number** | Put an honest cost on the chaos | ~15% |
| **5** | **The Myth** | What improvement content gets wrong about small business | ~10% |

**Pillar 1 is the reach pillar.** Recognition is what makes someone stop, and "that is
literally my Tuesday" is the reaction we are buying.

**Pillar 2 is the follow pillar.** Nobody follows an account for one good point. They
follow it for a series. The Translation posts are numbered deliberately so the series
is visible.

**Pillar 5 is the trust pillar.** Publishing where our own method does not apply builds
more credibility than ten posts claiming it always does.

## Hook discipline — the one rule that decides everything

**Slide 1 does all the work.** If slide 1 fails, slides 2–9 do not exist.

**A good slide 1 here:**
- Names a specific, recognizable situation — not a benefit, not a promise
- Is readable in under a second (≤ 10 words, big type)
- Implies the rest without summarizing it

| ✅ Works | ❌ Does not |
|---|---|
| "The job that got done twice." | "5 tips to improve your processes" |
| "Only Dave knows how to do that." | "Unlock operational excellence" |
| "The work takes an hour. The process takes 3 days." | "Lean can transform your business" |
| "Your team is not the problem." | "Are you struggling with inefficiency?" |

The right-hand column is what every other account in this space posts. That is the
opportunity.

**Never** open with a question the viewer can answer "no" to. "Are you struggling
with rework?" gives them permission to leave.

## Voice on social

Same as the brand: an operator talking to another operator. Short sentences, concrete
nouns, no jargon on the front slide.

**Additionally, on social:**
- **No hype punctuation.** No "🔥 THIS 🔥", no all-caps sentences, no emoji ladders.
- **No engagement bait.** No "comment YES if…", no fake polls, no "tag someone who".
- **No invented statistics.** If a number appears on a slide, it is either arithmetic
  the viewer can check, or it is labeled as an example.
- **No fake authority.** The account never says "we've helped hundreds of businesses"
  because we have not helped any yet.

That last constraint is a feature. An account in this niche that never inflates
anything will stand out inside a month.

## Cadence

| | TikTok | Instagram |
|---|---|---|
| Posts/week | 5 (Mon–Fri) | 5 (Mon–Fri) |
| Stories | — | 2–3/week, repurposed |
| Production | Batched — 10 posts per session, ~2 sessions/month |

**Same content on both**, with format tweaks (caption length, hashtag count, aspect
ratio). Cross-posting is a deliberate choice at this stage: two platforms of original
content is how one person burns out in six weeks.

Weekends are off. Consistency for a year beats intensity for a month.

## Posting times

**All times US Eastern.** The audience is in the US; you are in Jamaica. The slots are
set in the reader's clock, not yours.

| Platform | Slots (ET) |
|---|---|
| TikTok | 06:30 or 19:30 — before the working day, or after it |
| Instagram | 07:00 or 12:30 |

**Reasoning, not data:** the audience is running a business between 8am and 6pm. They
scroll before they start, at lunch, and after they have stopped. ⚠️ These are
hypotheses — replace them with your own analytics after 30 days.

**Set Buffer's timezone to `America/New_York`.** Jamaica is UTC−5 year-round; US
Eastern is UTC−4 from March to November. Anchoring to Eastern keeps the slot fixed for
the reader and lets Buffer handle daylight saving. Your own local time for a post
shifts by an hour twice a year, which costs nothing.

Eastern covers Central acceptably (06:30 ET = 05:30 CT). Pacific is poorly served; if
the data later shows a West Coast audience, add an afternoon slot rather than moving
this one.

## Market: North America / US

Decided, and it reaches further than the clock.

**What it settles:**
- **Pricing stands as drafted.** The $49 / $349 / $2,400 ladder was benchmarked against
  US and UK certification and consulting rates, so it is already aimed at this market.
- **Language is American English.** All 30 posts, the site and the lead magnet have
  been localized — this matters more than it sounds. The Callout pillar works entirely
  on recognition: "that is literally my Tuesday." A reader who registers the writer as
  foreign before they register the problem has already stopped reading. Words like
  *fortnight*, *on holiday* and *organised* break that spell instantly.
- **Examples are US-shaped**: apartments and elevators, not flats and lifts; a sheet of
  paper, not A4; vacation, not holiday.

**What it leaves open:**
- **The vertical (C4)** is still unchosen. US trades and home services is the strongest
  candidate — large, reachable on these platforms, and the waste is physical and
  visible. Not yet decided.
- **Regional accent within the US.** Nothing in the content is region-specific, which
  is fine to start.

**What it does not change:** the faceless posture. Selling US small businesses from
Jamaica is a non-issue for digital products, and for coaching the brand is faceless
anyway — the named human appears at the discovery call, where trust is actually built.
If anything the geography argues *for* the faceless model rather than against it.

## What social is NOT for

- **Not for selling coaching.** Wrong surface, wrong trust level.
- **Not for DM outreach.** Cold DMs will get the account restricted and are a worse
  version of the email outreach already planned.
- **Not for follower count.** The only metrics that matter are saves, sends, and
  profile-link clicks. A 2,000-follower account that sends 40 people a month to the
  Waste Walk beats a 50,000-follower account that sends none.

## How we will know it is working

Reviewed at day 30, against thresholds committed **now**:

| Signal | Threshold | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **Saves + sends** per post | Top 20% of posts clear 3% of views | The content is useful, not just watchable |
| **Profile → link clicks** | ≥ 2% of profile visits | The bio and CTA are working |
| **Waste Walk opt-ins from social** | ≥ 25 in month 1 | The funnel connects at all |
| **Which pillar wins** | Any clear winner | Rebuild the calendar around it |
| Followers | *Not a threshold* | Tracked, not optimized for |

**If opt-ins are under 10 after 30 days** with reasonable reach, the problem is the
bridge — bio, CTA or landing page — not the content. Fix that before making more.

**If reach itself is near zero**, the bet at the top of this file is wrong: this
audience is not reachable this way, and the effort should move to search-intent
content where the earlier research is stronger.

Either outcome is worth knowing after 30 days of cheap slideshows. That is the point
of running it this way.
