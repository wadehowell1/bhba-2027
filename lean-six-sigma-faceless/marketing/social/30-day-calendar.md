Mon–Fri, weekends off · **Weeks:** 6 weekdays-per-week grid below runs Day 1 = first

caption, hashtags, the CTA and any asset notes. Slide copy is final — it goes onto the
image as written.

`production-system.md`.

## Day 5 — An hour of work, three days of process
**Pillar:** Number · **Format:** 8 slides · **CTA:** soft

| # | Slide copy |
|---|---|
| 1 | **The work takes an hour. The process takes three days.** |
| 2 | Time the actual work. Usually about an hour. |
| 3 | Now time the whole thing, start to finish. Three days. |
| 4 | So where did the other 23 hours go? |
| 5 | Sat in an inbox. Waiting on a decision. Waiting on a callback. |
| 6 | Here is why that matters: |
| 7 | Making the hour 20% faster changes almost nothing. |
| 8 | **You optimized the hour and ignored the three days.** |

**Caption:**
> This is the single most common pattern I see, and it's the one that redirects where
> people look.
>
> When a job takes too long the instinct is to look at the person doing it. Work
> faster, be more organized. But if 90% of the elapsed time is waiting, then the person
> doing the work was never the problem.
>
> Time both next week. The gap is the whole answer.

## Day 10 — The ten-minute meeting
**Pillar:** Fix · **Format:** 9 slides · **CTA:** link

| # | Slide copy |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Ten minutes a day. Standing up.** |
| 2 | Not a meeting. Not an update. Not a status report. |
| 3 | Three questions, same time every day: |
| 4 | **1.** What is going out today? |
| 5 | **2.** What is going to stop us? |
| 6 | **3.** What went wrong yesterday? |
| 7 | Ten minutes. Standing. Same place. |
| 8 | Problems surface on Tuesday — |
| 9 | **instead of on the invoice.** |

**Caption:**
> The highest-return ten minutes in a small business, and the one most likely to get
> dropped in week three.
>
> Rules that make it survive: stand up, keep it to ten, and never use it to solve
> anything. It's for surfacing, not fixing. The moment it becomes a problem-solving
> meeting it becomes forty minutes and people start finding reasons to miss it.
