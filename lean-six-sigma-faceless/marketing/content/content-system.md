# Content System

## Channel strategy — pick where the customer already is

> The framework's warning applies directly here: choose channels by where the audience
> already spends time, not by what is trendy. A B2B service and a consumer app do not
> belong on the same channels.

| Channel | Serves | Why | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| **YouTube / search-intent video** | Practitioner + Operator | Evergreen, faceless-native (screen + diagram), found at the moment of the trigger | **1** |
| **Written SEO articles** | Practitioner | The template/tool searches are real and evergreen | **1** |
| **Email list** | Both | The only audience we own. Everything routes here | **1** |
| **Short-form (Reels/TikTok/Shorts)** | Operator | Reach, and operators do scroll. Clips cut from long-form | 2 |
| **Direct outreach** | Operator | The only reliable way to sell the first coaching engagements | 2 |
| **LinkedIn** | Practitioner | Where ops people talk shop | 3 |
| **Trade communities / forums** | Operator | High trust, low volume, easy to get thrown out of | 3 — participate honestly, do not spam |
| **Paid ads** | — | Not until Gate 3. No CAC data; do not buy traffic to an unvalidated funnel | Later |

> ⚠️ **Test 1 (keyword research) decides the balance between practitioner and operator
> content.** Do not commit a calendar before running it.

## Cadence — realistic, not aspirational

One long-form piece per week (article + matching video), three shorts cut from it, one
email. Consistency beats volume; a channel that publishes weekly for a year beats one
that publishes daily for six weeks and stops.

## Format rules (faceless)

- Screen recordings, diagrams, whiteboard-style animation, and B-roll of real work.
- Voiceover is fine. If synthetic voice is used, **say so once in the channel
  description.** Do not pretend a synthetic voice is a person.
- Never fabricate a client story to illustrate a point. Use clearly-labeled worked
  examples ("here's a made-up plumbing firm with eleven people") — illustration is
  honest, invented evidence is not.

# 10 Short-Form Hooks

1. "Your business doesn't have a people problem. It has a process nobody wrote down."
2. "If the answer to 'how often does that go wrong' is 'a lot' — you've found it."
3. "Three days to do one hour of work. Here's where the other 23 went."
4. "Stop calling it a quality issue. Nobody wakes up wanting to do it badly."
5. "The most expensive person in your business is the only one who knows how."
6. "You don't need a certificate. You need Tuesday back."
7. "Everything written about lean assumes you have a thousand staff. You have eleven."
8. "Watch what actually happens, not what's supposed to happen. That gap is the money."
9. "If your team thinks this is about their jobs, it will not work. Say so out loud."
10. "One process. Not the business. One."

# 10 Educational Posts

1. **The seven wastes, renamed** — every classic waste in words a shop floor uses.
2. **The two-week tally sheet** — how to get data when you have none.
3. **How to write standard work the doer will actually follow** — write it with them.
4. **The huddle script** — the exact ten-minute agenda, three questions.
5. **Root cause vs. the thing that annoyed you** — worked example.
6. **Why your SOPs are not being read** — usually too long, wrong place, wrong author.
7. **What "one piece flow" means when you're a two-van operation.**
8. **The five stalls** — why improvement projects die in weeks 2–3, and what to do.
9. **Measuring before/after honestly** — including why "hours saved × 52" is not cash.
10. **When lean is the wrong answer** — if the problem is demand, pricing or the
    wrong customers, no amount of process work fixes it.

> Post 10 matters. Publishing where your own method does not apply builds more trust
> than ten posts claiming it always does.

# 5 Case Study Concepts

> ⚠️ **These are structures to fill in when real engagements exist.** Every one of
> these requires a real client, real numbers, and written permission. Publishing any
> of these with invented figures would be fabricating evidence — which the project
> rules forbid and which is the fastest way to lose this audience.

1. **The callback** — a trade business, callback rate before/after, over 90 days.
2. **The handover** — a process that only one person could do, and what changed when
   three could.
3. **The quote** — where quoting errors entered, and what the fix was worth.
4. **The failed one** — an engagement that did not work, and why. *(Publish this one.
   It will out-perform the other four for trust.)*
5. **The two-week tally** — a business that had no data, and what counting revealed.

# CTA Ideas

**Primary, everywhere:** "Run the free Waste Walk on one process. One hour."

Others by context:
- End of a practitioner article: "The filled-in version of this template is in the Kit."
- End of an operator video: "Pick the one that annoyed you most this week. Start there."
- Email: "Reply and tell me what you found — I read them all."
- High intent only: "Six places a quarter. See if it fits."

**Never used:** countdown timers, "only 3 spots left" when there are more, "limited
time" on an evergreen product, or any urgency that is not literally true.
